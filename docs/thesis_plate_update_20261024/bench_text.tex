\subsection{Seeing the plate conform in PyBullet}
\label{sec:plate-visual-bench}

Figure~\ref{fig:plate-conformity-bullet} adds an isolated contact bench using the floating-plate implementation, with the geometry and default stiffnesses stated above. The three prescribed surfaces are a flat face, a face tilted by $12^\circ$ and a convex body with two front facets. The flange translates along the push axis while its orientation is held fixed. Gravity is disabled to isolate this contact response. The plate collision is disabled, so all object load passes through the three sphere links. No SOCP, unknown-object estimator or wrist controller is used in this illustration.

\thesisfigure[1.0]{ch04_contacts/plate_conformity_bullet.pdf}{Recorded PyBullet poses at the first loaded contact state (top) and after settling (bottom), using one fixed camera. Blue is the flange and its rigid supports, gold the rigid floating plate, orange the three spheres and grey the contacted body. In the faceted case, two grey tints distinguish the actual front facets. A pad is counted as loaded above $0.05$ N. The springs are numerical force elements and are explained in the side-view schematic, rather than rendered as physical coils.}{fig:plate-conformity-bullet}

Each trial lasts $3$ s at a $0.25$ ms physics step. A smooth $1.4$ s approach ends $1.2$ mm beyond the average nominal sphere tangency for that surface, followed by a hold. Peripheral stiffness is $500$ N/m in each direction; the central translation is $20000$ N/m, central torsion $40$ Nm/rad and central tilt stiffness zero. Contact stiffness and damping are $8000$ N/m and $207$ N\,s/m, with friction $0.7$. These are the general floating-plate defaults, distinct from the $2700$ N/m central axial spring in the later Kinova model.

The flat case has three loaded pads from its first recorded loaded state. The tilted and faceted cases initially have two and finish with three. On the $12^\circ$ face the plate settles at approximately $11.0^\circ$ relative to the flange. Figure~\ref{fig:plate-conformity-trace} shows that this is passive conformity accompanied by unequal contact loading: the final normal forces are approximately $0.24$, $6.33$ and $6.64$ N. Counting three contacts does not mean that all three carry the same force or have a large friction reserve.

\thesisfigure[0.94]{ch04_contacts/plate_conformity_trace.pdf}{The tilted-face bench. Top: actual normal force on each sphere link. Bottom: plate tilt relative to the fixed-attitude flange, compared with the $12^\circ$ face. The dotted line marks the end of commanded approach; the remaining interval is a hold. The plate acquires contact through its passive degrees of freedom, without a command to its individual spheres.}{fig:plate-conformity-trace}

These prescribed surfaces illustrate the mechanism; they are not a population success rate or an insertion test. Only the plate pose and the three contact resultants are measured here. The archived geometry campaign in Chapter~\ref{ch:geometry} evaluates more varied shapes and the complete manipulation sequence. The figure data, copied model hashes and plotting script are listed in the figure manifest.
